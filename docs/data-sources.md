# Data sources

Map Vietnam ships no maps or record databases (only the catalogue of where to fetch them, and
small excerpts for the tests). `python3 start.py` downloads each record source from its
publisher onto your computer the first time it runs, and keeps the download in `data/raw/` so it
is not fetched again unless you ask (most ingesters take `--refresh`). Old maps are downloaded only when you add one (the viewer's "Add old maps",
or `python3 start.py maps add`), and removing a map deletes its download too. This page says, for each source, what it is, where it comes from, its licence, how
its records are placed on the map, and what is left out.

Every record keeps a link to its source. Where a source's licence asks for credit, the credit
line is in [NOTICE](../NOTICE).

| Part (`--only` name) | Source | Licence | Key | Download |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `gns` | NGA GEOnet Names Server | US Government work | No | 8 MB |
| `hes_gazetteer` | NARA Hamlet Evaluation System gazetteers | Public domain | No | 10 MB |
| `datum_check` | (no download: a check on the two above) | — | No | — |
| `caccf` | NARA Combat Area Casualties Current File | Public domain | No | 13 MB |
| `wiki_mia` | English Wikipedia missing-in-action lists | CC BY-SA 4.0 | No | 1 MB |
| `vn_relics` | Vietnamese Wikipedia national relic lists | CC BY-SA 4.0 (+ CC0, ODbL) | No | 30 MB |
| `wikidata_osm` | Wikidata and OpenStreetMap | CC0; ODbL 1.0 | No | 10 MB |
| `thor` | THOR air operations (Kaggle) | Public domain / CC0 | Free Kaggle key | 320 MB |
| `vhpa` | Vietnam Helicopter Pilots Association (optional) | Copyrighted | No | 20 MB, 3–4 hours |

`python3 start.py setup --only <part>` runs one part again, plus anything unfinished it depends on.

## Maps

Every map that can be added is listed, with its download addresses and size, in
`data/sources/map_library.json` (generated by `scripts/map_library_catalogue.py`). Nothing in it is
downloaded until someone adds that map.

| Series | Maps | Typical size | Holder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| US Army Map Service L7014 (with border sheets L7015, L7016), 1:50,000 | 498 sheets, almost the whole country | about 10 MB to download, about 20 MB on disk once added; all 498: about 5 GB to download, 11 GB on disk | USGS Store |
| Joint Operations Graphic (Air) 1501, 1:250,000 | 32 sheets, an overview of the whole country | about 10 MB to download, about 30 MB on disk | USGS Store; University of Texas Libraries as a fallback |
| Older scanned maps | 7 maps, 1873–1962, Mekong Delta | 2–70 MB each | five map libraries, see below |

"On disk" counts the map's tiles and its downloaded file, which is kept so the map can be rebuilt
without downloading it again. Removing a map deletes both. The sizes the viewer and
`python3 start.py maps list` show come from the same catalogue.

### Coverage

The USGS Store hosts 501 of the 816 L7014 cells (15′ squares) that touch Vietnam: 472 of series
L7014, 22 border sheets of the Laos series L7015 and 7 of the Cambodia series L7016, which carry
the Vietnamese side of the border. Three of the 501 cannot be used (below), so 498 can be added.
Most of the 315 cells with no sheet are open sea. About 110 hold land, mostly along the borders
and on islands:

- **North:** the China border from Lào Cai to Hà Giang and Cao Bằng (including the far northern
  tip around Đồng Văn), the Laos border in Lai Châu, Điện Biên and Sơn La, one inland sheet near
  Sơn La (5851 III), and the Quảng Ninh coast and islands around Móng Cái and Cẩm Phả.
- **Centre:** strips along the Laos border from Thanh Hóa to Kon Tum, a few inland cells in Thanh
  Hóa and Nghệ An, the Cambodian border in Bình Phước and Tây Ninh, and some islands (Lý Sơn).
- **South:** the Cambodian border from Tây Ninh to Hà Tiên, the south of Phú Quốc and the islands
  around it (Thổ Chu, Hòn Sơn), parts of the Cà Mau peninsula, Côn Đảo and Phú Quý.

The 1:250,000 Joint Operations Graphics fill most of these gaps at less detail. Places neither
series covers: the far north along the China border (Lào Cai, Hà Giang, northern Cao Bằng and
Lạng Sơn, around Sơn La), the Móng Cái tip, the Laos border in western Nghệ An, Phú Quốc's
southern half with the islands west of Rạch Giá, and Phú Quý island. The JOG sheets for Lào Cai
(NF 48-6), Yên Bái (NF 48-10), the far north (NF 48-2, 3, 4), eastern Cao Bằng (NF 48-8) and
Kampot with Phú Quốc (NC 48-5) are not on the USGS Store, so they are not offered.

### US Army Map Service L7014, 1:50,000 (mostly 1965–1970)

GeoPDFs from the USGS Store (`store.usgs.gov/assets/MOD/StoreFiles/NGA/`, with
`store.usgs.gov/assets/yimages/NGA/` as a second address). Each sheet carries its own
georeferencing (registration points, or on some reissues only a page-to-grid transform), so it is
placed without any hand work, then cut at its printed frame. The sheets are on the Indian 1960
datum and sit within about 50 m of modern imagery once converted. Some sheets' georeferencing
names another datum (`IND-I`, `INF-A`); their margins say Indian 1960, and they are read as such
(see [known-limits.md](known-limits.md)). Public domain as works of the US Government.

Sheet numbers follow the series: each 4-digit sheet covers 30′ × 30′, and the fifth digit is the
quarter (1 = NE, 2 = SE, 3 = SW, 4 = NW). For example Cao Lãnh is sheet 6129 IV, file
`L701461294_geo.pdf`. Three hosted sheets are left out because they cannot be used as the USGS
Store serves them: 6731 I (both copies broken), 6836 I (its georeferencing places it in the
wrong country) and the Laos border sheet 5949 IV (no georeferencing in the PDF).

### Joint Operations Graphic (Air) 1501, 1:250,000 (about 1966–2000)

An overview series: each sheet covers 1.5° of longitude by 1° of latitude (about 160 by 110 km),
so 32 sheets cover nearly the whole country, including most places the 1:50,000 sheets miss.
They are drawn for pilots, with less detail than the 1:50,000 sheets; expect them to sit
about 100–200 m from today's map. GeoPDFs from the USGS Store; the University of Texas Libraries host the
same files and are tried when the Store fails (credit them when a file comes from there; see
[NOTICE](../NOTICE)). Most sheets are on Indian 1960; three later reprints are on WGS84. The sheet
layout is irregular, so each sheet's area comes from its own PDF. Each sheet is cut at its printed
frame, so the white margin of the scan is left out. Many sheets are editions from the 1970s or
later reprints, so check a sheet's date (shown in the viewer) before reading it as a wartime map.
Public domain as works of the US Government.

Some GeoPDFs of both US series carry the release code "DS" or "OO" in their metadata. The
sheets are US Government works and freely downloadable; check the printed margin before
redistributing tiles made from them.

Two of these are non-commercial licences: you may use the maps for research and share what you
make from them with credit, but not sell it. The scans are placed by hand-picked control points
(`data/scans/<id>.json`), usually where printed latitude and longitude lines cross. Their surveys
are much older than the 1960s sheets: expect errors of hundreds of metres, and of 1–5 km on the
1873 and 1889 maps. Each map's date note in the viewer gives what was measured.

## Records

### NGA GEOnet Names Server (`gns`)

The US gazetteer of foreign place names: every approved, variant and older name of each feature
in Vietnam, in WGS84. The variant names matter, because many are the 1960s spellings without
diacritics that wartime reports use. Each name becomes a `placename` record, and the ingester
writes a name index that other ingesters use to place records that give only a place name.

The live site failed a security check (DNSSEC) when this was written, so the ingester downloads a
pinned Internet Archive capture of the country file, byte for byte the original. Cemeteries,
tombs and named graves are left out. US Government work; NGA asks for the credit in NOTICE.

### NARA Hamlet Evaluation System gazetteers (`hes_gazetteer`)

Three fixed-width files from the US National Archives (Record Group 472, catalog ids 4628955,
4626239 and 4628241): the hamlets of South Vietnam in 1972–73, with their wartime province,
district and village, and a 100 m grid reference. The grid is decoded on Indian 1960 (see
`datum_check` below). Only these static files are fetched; the archive's `robots.txt` forbids
crawling its catalogue. The ingester also writes a covering circle for every wartime province and
district, used to place records that name only a province. Public domain.

### Datum check (`datum_check`)

No download. It pairs HES hamlets with the same hamlets in the GNS gazetteer and measures which
datum the wartime grid references agree with. The answer, Indian 1960, is what every ingester
uses for zoneless wartime grids. Output: `data/work/datum_calibration.json`.

### NARA Combat Area Casualties Current File (`caccf`)

The US Department of Defense casualty file, December 1998 extract (Record Group 330, catalog id
1965870): one row per US service member who died in the war. Only records whose body was not
recovered are kept. The file has no grid or place name, only a province code, so each record is
placed at the centre of its province with a circle covering the whole province, or of its
military region or country when that is all it gives. Service numbers are never stored, because
many are Social Security numbers. Public domain.

### Wikipedia missing-in-action lists (`wiki_mia`)

The five English Wikipedia pages "List of United States servicemembers and civilians missing in
action during the Vietnam War", read as raw wikitext. Only people still unaccounted for are kept.
The pages give place names, not coordinates, so each record's circle covers the place named (a
province, a district, or a village, base or valley found in the GNS gazetteer). CC BY-SA 4.0;
every record links its page.

### Vietnamese national relic lists (`vn_relics`)

The Vietnamese Wikipedia lists of national and special national relics, plus their article
coordinates, Wikidata district membership (CC0) and OpenStreetMap province borders (ODbL). Only
war-related sites from 1858–1975 are kept: battles, massacres, resistance bases, prisons, posts,
tunnels and shoot-down sites. Cemeteries, graves, tombs and temples are left out. The lists name
places in the merged administrative units of 2025, while the gazetteer has the older names, so the
geocoding is careful to cover every place a row names. CC BY-SA 4.0.

### Wikidata and OpenStreetMap (`wikidata_osm`)

Two sources built together:

- **Wikidata** (CC0): battles, sieges, operations, massacres, air strikes, aircraft losses,
  military bases, forts and airfields in or near Vietnam, dated 1858–1989. Coordinates are
  article-level, one point for a whole event, so the circles are wide.
- **OpenStreetMap** (ODbL 1.0, © OpenStreetMap contributors): historic military places,
  battlefields, forts, bunkers, war memorials and old airstrips, plus Vietnam's national boundary.

Present-day military installations and formal burials are left out. OpenStreetMap is queried
through the public Overpass servers, which are often busy; if this part fails, run it again later.

### THOR air operations (`thor`)

The US Air Force's Theater History of Operations database for Vietnam: one row per strike
mission, 1965–1973, published on Kaggle (`usaf/vietnam-war-bombing-operations`). Public domain;
the Kaggle copy is marked CC0. Kaggle needs a free account and API key (see the [user guide](guide.md#air-strike-records)); once the
320 MB zip is downloaded, no key is needed again.

Positions are re-derived from the original grid reference on Indian 1960 when there is one,
because THOR's own WGS84 columns are off by up to about 300 m. A THOR point is the target, not
where the bombs fell, so no strike has a radius under 300 m. Only strike missions are kept.

By default only strikes in the Cao Lãnh area (105.35–105.95 E, 10.15–10.75 N) are loaded. To load
another area, or the whole country (a much bigger database), run the ingester directly after
setup:

```sh
.venv/bin/python scripts/ingest/thor.py --bbox 106.0,10.5,107.0,11.5
.venv/bin/python scripts/ingest/thor.py --all-vietnam
```

On Windows, use `.venv\Scripts\python` instead of `.venv/bin/python`.

`--bbox` takes west,south,east,north in degrees. Each run replaces the stored strikes with the
ones inside the new area.

### Vietnam Helicopter Pilots Association (`vhpa`, optional)

The VHPA website's pages on helicopter losses: one incident page per aircraft lost, and pages for
the pilots. The site is copyrighted, so only short facts are stored (date, aircraft, unit, grid,
outcome, status codes), for personal research; no narrative text is stored or quoted. The site
has no `robots.txt`; the ingester reads one page every 3 seconds, so a full download takes 3 to 4
hours. It needs the `gns`, `hes_gazetteer` and `wikidata_osm` parts first. Turn it on with
`python3 start.py setup --only vhpa`.
